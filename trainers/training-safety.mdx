You're expected to use reasonable care and professional judgment in every Drill and GameCheck. That starts with each Hooper's stated skill level, age, goals, and any limitations they've told you about.

## Designing Drills

- Don't give a Hooper an exercise that isn't right for their ability, age, or limitations.
- Add warm-up guidance for explosive movement, conditioning, or repetitive high-intensity work.
- Explain technique and progression when an exercise has a foreseeable safety risk, and offer an easier version for younger or less experienced Hoopers.
- Remind Hoopers to train somewhere safe with enough room and a suitable surface. Never in a street, a parking lot with traffic, or another unsafe spot.
- Avoid weighted, plyometric, or conditioning loads that don't fit the Hooper's age or experience.

If a video shows an unsafe space or a technique with real injury risk, address it in your GameCheck.

## Pain, injuries, and emergencies

You're a coach, not a medical provider. Don't diagnose injuries or recommend treatment, supplements, or medication.

- **Significant pain, dizziness, chest pain, shortness of breath, or a head injury:** tell the Hooper to stop.
- **A significant injury or medical concern:** recommend they see a qualified medical professional. If they're a minor, recommend they tell a parent or guardian.
- **A medical emergency:** tell the Hooper to call 911 or their local emergency number right away, then notify Hooper Howse.

## Insurance

Insurance isn't required to coach on Hooper Howse, but you're encouraged to consider coverage for your training business.
